# Review of the IEEE paper v2 against submission standards (29 Oct 2026)

Paper: *Does Crowd Evidence Help Flood-Aware Routing? An Offline Routing Prototype and a Replay Study on the Chennai Road Network*
(`latex/paper.tex`, `CityPulse_paper_v2.pdf`, 8 pages, 90 references). Reviewed from the LaTeX source and the PDF; the PDF could not be
rebuilt here (no TeX installation on this laptop).

## Verdict

**Well written and honest, but not ready to submit.** The writing, structure, IEEE formatting and scientific framing are at a good
conference standard, and the paper already follows the project's wording rules (index not bound, no device claim, ALT not used,
no novelty claim). It fails on **currency**: its numbers and its description of the method come from before the 2 October fixes, and
the project's own newer results (which strengthen the conclusion) are missing. It also has unfinished front matter.

## 4. What is already strong

- The research question is sharp and the answer is honest, including the negative results.
- The common reference belief, the observed/prior-only split, the hybrid baseline and the held-out check are the right controls.
- Threats to validity are specific; limitations appear in the abstract.
- The prose is clear and free of overclaiming.

## 5. Suggested path to v3

1. Fix B1 to B4 together: regenerate `numbers.tex` and the tables and figures from the 2 Oct results, rewrite Section III around the
   Beta index, turn Section VII into "fixed and re-run".
2. Add S4 (ADR-036) and S1 (availability).
3. Fill in the front matter and the AI disclosure (B5, B6), verify the three references (B7).
4. Choose the venue (S9), then trim references (S8) and adjust length.
5. Rebuild the PDF (needs a TeX installation or Overleaf) and run `check_citations.py`.
