## What was found

- The existing verifier rejected 166 of 432 first-draft answer/situation/language combinations; every rejection was an English sentence opener (or a banned word such as "it works") that rules 2 and 6 cannot ground. Rewording fixed all; the verifier was not changed.
- The cue lists reach 100% on the dev set only because they were adjusted on it. That figure is not a result.

## 15 October: what "satisfactory" means for run1, written before any evaluation result exists

The owner is away and asked whether the result is satisfactory. These are the readings fixed in advance; they say whether to **continue** (export, quantise, blind ratings, phone test), never whether to **adopt** (that needs the pre-registered sealed test set, blind ratings, faithfulness audit and phone ceilings).

- **Understanding (task U), 300 validation questions:** intent accuracy at least 0.90 overall and at least 0.85 on the Tamil questions; unparseable replies at most 2%. The validation questions come from the same bank as the training questions, so even a pass overstates generalisation.
- **Writing (task W), 60 validation answers, given the gold intents:** at least 90% pass the verifier; **every** safety question is refused; **every** low- or stale-confidence answer carries the hedge. The reference is the untouched base model (0 of 10 passed).
- **Reading:** the first 12 answers are read by the coding agent for fluency and faithfulness (not blind; informal, noted as such).
- **If the readings are missed:** the result is reported as it is; the settings are not tuned and rerun (addendum 2). A second run is allowed only to fix a bug, and then both runs are reported.
- **If they are met:** "promising", nothing more. The model still has to beat D0 version 2 in the blind pairwise ratings by at least three team members before it is adopted.

## 15 October: run1 results, read against the criteria above (nothing was tuned or rerun)

Run `run1`: Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, LoRA r16 on all attention and MLP projections, 6,000 labelling (U) plus 2,400 answer-writing (W) examples, 1 epoch, 525 steps, 223.6 minutes on the GTX 1650, final training loss about 0.012 to 0.017 (a training figure, not a quality figure). Checkpoints saved every 100 steps (`run1/checkpoints/step_100` to `step_500` and `final`); the adapter is 34 MB. Evaluation: `data/qa/2026-10-13/runs/run1/eval.json`, `run1.eval.out`, `eval_samples.md`. The untouched base model was evaluated on the same questions as the reference (`run1_base`).

| Criterion (fixed before the result) | Required | Run1 | Met? | Untouched base |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| U: intent accuracy, 300 validation questions | at least 0.90 | 0.9967 (299 of 300) | yes | 0.0 |
| U: accuracy on the Tamil questions | at least 0.85 | 0.9924 | yes | |
| U: unparseable replies | at most 2% | 0% | yes | 100% |
| W: answers passing the verifier, 60 | at least 90% | **88.3% (53 of 60)** | **no, narrowly** | 10% (6 of 60) |
| W: every safety question refused | all | 8 of 8 | yes | 0 of 8 |
| W: every low or stale answer hedged | all | 32 of 32 | yes | 0 of 32 |

**Verdict against the pre-declared readings: not met, because one criterion (verifier pass rate 88.3% against 90%) was missed.** It is reported as it is. The settings were not tuned and no second run was made.

What the numbers do and do not say:
- **All seven verifier failures are numerals the route does not support** (for example "Route A is 4 minutes faster", "Hazard delays add about 15 minutes", "10 years old"); two also carry a wrong comparative direction. The verifier is doing its job: a model answer with an unsupported number is rejected and the app would show the D0 answer instead. No unsafe or unhedged claim got through in this sample. This is the failure the design expects of a 0.5 B model: it writes fluent text and gets figures wrong.
- **Understanding is strong but overstated.** 299 of 300 on questions drawn from the same bank as the training questions. The rule classifier scored 0.91 against the same gold labels on the same rows, but the gold labels came from the generator, so this is not an independent comparison. The one error was a Tamil two-part question (tunnel open? plus what was avoided) where it answered the second part as primary and the safety question as secondary.
- **The model mostly reproduces D0.** 60% of its answers are word-for-word one of the training styles; its lexical variety (distinct-1 0.12, distinct-2 0.25) is below the untouched base model (0.27, 0.69) and, by construction, bounded by the templates it learned from. Only the first four samples in `eval_samples.md` were read in full (informal, not blind, by the coding agent; the pre-declared twelve were not completed): the text was fluent and, in the examples read, identical to the D0 style shown beside it. So the fine-tune has so far bought an answer writer that is no richer than D0 and sometimes wrong on numbers.
- **What this does not show:** nothing about unseen question phrasings (no sealed test set yet), nothing about Tamil answer quality (Tamil answers were excluded from training and are unreviewed), nothing about speed or memory on the phone (not measured).

Reading for the decision: the label task (D1, small model labels the question, D0 writes the answer) looks worth taking forward, since it is accurate and every answer would still come from the verified templates. The answer-writing task (D2) has not earned a place: it is no more varied than D0 and fails the verifier on numerals 11.7% of the time. Recommendation, pending the owner: continue with D1 export and phone measurement; do not spend effort on D2 until a sealed test set exists. Any change to the training data (for example copying the numbers into the answer by rule rather than by model) would be a new, pre-registered run, reported beside this one.
