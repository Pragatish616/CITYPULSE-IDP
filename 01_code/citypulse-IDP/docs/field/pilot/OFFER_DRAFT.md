## The problem you already have

When Chennai's subways flood, the city's traffic police and corporation post closures as text, and people piece them together from several places
(our market notes, `06_research_notes/market.md`, record this for the 2024 cyclone). In the sources we found, none gives you, subway by subway,
**what someone saw there and when**, or says plainly that **nobody has looked**.

## What the pilot gives you

For each subway on the Greater Chennai Corporation's own list (16 road/rail and 5 pedestrian subways, plus 10 more named in the news or on the map):

- the newest observation: *seen passable*, *seen not passable* (with a depth estimate), or *could not tell*, with the **time and its age**;
- how many people have logged it, and a flag when two observers disagree;
- an explicit **"No observation"** when no one has logged it, kept apart from "could not tell";
- a daily file (CSV) and a one-page board during the monsoon season, and a short written coverage summary each week: which subways were covered, which were not.

## What it is not

- It is **not a safe-route or "go / no-go" service**. It records what people saw and when. It never says a road is safe or passable.
- It is **not official data** and not a sensor feed. Observations are volunteers' with phone clocks.
- Positions of the subways come from the corporation's table and OpenStreetMap and are being confirmed on a map; **none is verified on the ground yet**.
- No coverage is promised. Some subways may have no observations on some days.
