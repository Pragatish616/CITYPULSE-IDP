# Urban flooding in Chennai and Indian cities: road mobility, warning systems, ground truth

Scope note: these notes cover Chennai 2015, 2021 and 2023 (Michaung); street-level and urban flood modelling and forecasting for Indian cities; Indian warning and navigation products; Indian crowd-sourced flood mapping; and mobility impacts. I left out works already in /home/claude/citypulse_context/refs.tsv: radhakrishnan2024chennai, devi2019sprawl, seenirajan2017chennai, saharia2021inventory, ramasamy2018geoanthropogenic, vanoldenborgh2016chennai, nearing2024global. Some findings below still lean on them, and each such use is labelled "(already in refs.tsv)".

How sources were verified. I opened each paper's Crossref record with an api.crossref.org query fetched through Exa, because the Bash egress proxy blocks api.crossref.org. The verdicts mean the following:
- **VERIFIED**: title, authors, venue, volume, issue, pages and DOI were all read off the Crossref record.
- **PARTIAL**: some fields could not be confirmed. The row says which.
- Summaries come from the abstracts I read through Consensus and Exa, not from full text, unless stated otherwise.

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- The peer-reviewed Chennai literature is mostly rainfall meteorology (rows 1–4), basin hydraulics and SAR at catchment scale (5, 17–19), and susceptibility mapping (16). Street-segment, time-resolved outputs are rare. The exceptions are crowdsourced (9, 22, O7, O9).
- The Indian road-flood literature (12–15) is scenario-based (design storms), not event-replay. That leaves room for CityPulse to be evaluated on real event data.

## Q2. Where and when Chennai roads flooded in 2015 and 2023, at what resolution, and what is public

### Takeaway
Street-level, time-stamped records exist only from crowdsourcing. 2015 has a binary flooded-street layer (OSM ways, a single snapshot, no depth). The 2022–2025 CFM-DSS crowd layer has timestamps, levels 1–6 **and negative "no water" reports**, and so may be the closest thing to independent ground truth for a router. Depth ground truth from field surveys exists in the papers (NCCR's more than 400 points; the Adyar floodplain depths in row 5; Karmegam's field data) but I found no public download.

### Cited Findings
- 2015 rainfall: more than 1,200 mm from 5 Nov to 10 Dec 2015, beating the 24-h record (290 mm) on 1–2 Dec — [Panda & Rath 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00024-022-03113-w).
- 2015 impacts: 289 deaths and 23.25 lakh houses inundated; air, rail and road transport halted for several days — [CAG Report 4 of 2017](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2017/Report_No_4_of_2017_-_Performance_Audit_of_Flood_Management_and_Response_in_Chennai_and_its_Suburban_Area.pdf). Note that [Ramasamy et al. 2018 (already in refs.tsv)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11069-018-3213-3) gives 421 deaths, so counts differ by source and boundary.
- 2015 street-level crowd data: more than 2,500 streets reported flooded within 24 h through an OSM-based tool. Data is OSM-way-level and binary (`is_flooded`), anonymous, unversioned and unverified — [Naik 2016](https://doi.org/10.1109/SysEng.2016.7753186); [osm-in/flood-map](https://github.com/osm-in/flood-map); [Mapbox blog](https://blog.mapbox.com/crowdsourcing-flood-data-for-chennai-44353378317d).
- 2015 depth points: 95 social-media water-height points (72 in Chennai), validated against field data at RMSE ±0.3 m — [Karmegam et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40677-021-00195-x).
- 2015 field flood levels: NCCR mapped flood levels at more than 400 field points, which were used to validate C-FLOWS — [Times of India 2018](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/chennai/ai-based-system-to-forecast-flood-in-city-week-in-advance/articleshow/65814959.cms).
- 2005 and 2015 measured flood depths across the Adyar floodplain, used for MIKE FLOOD validation (R² 0.883 for 2015) — [Ajith Kumar et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44327-026-00238-8).
- Historic flood points: 123 flood-reported locations in the GCC area — [Natarajan et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12524-021-01331-8).
- 2015 extents: UNOSAT (28 Nov) and ISRO RISAT-1 (3 Dec) inundation layers — [osm-in/flood-map](https://github.com/osm-in/flood-map). An NRSC 2015 flood-extent layer is in the CFM-DSS mirror — [HF chennaidss-gis-layers](https://huggingface.co/datasets/CashlessConsumer/chennaidss-gis-layers). Reservoir discharges exceeded 35,000 m³/s in 2015 and 15,000 m³/s in 2023 per [Radhakrishnan et al. 2024 (already in refs.tsv)](https://doi.org/10.3390/w16172477).
- 2023 (Michaung): close to 500 mm in about a day at Nungambakkam and Meenambakkam, and more than 600 mm in the north; 17 deaths — [OpenCity datajam, Jan 2024](https://opencity.in/chennai-rains-and-waterlogging-datajam-jan-2024/). Regional rainfall intensity reached 164.26 mm/h on 4 Dec — [Mishra et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2024.122369).
- 2023 crowd depths: about 1,200 users logged waterlogging depths on 4–5 Dec 2023 via chennaiwaterlogging.org — [Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/iitm-team-log-flood-water-levels-chennai-residents-forecasting/).
- CFM-DSS crowd layer: records carry an `imagetimestamp` from 2022-11-14 to 2025-12-04, `level` 1–6, a note and a point. They include explicit negatives such as "No water - Walajaha Road" (2022-11-14), "no water in OMR near Sholinganallur tank" (2025-11-14) and "No inundation in ECR Vettuvankenni" (2025-11-26). Some are tests ("Test", "test case") and some fall outside Chennai (lon 77.72) — [HF chennai-flood-history](https://huggingface.co/datasets/CashlessConsumer/chennai-flood-history).
- GCC rain-gauge time series by zone, starting 2020-11-03 — [HF chennai-rain-gauges](https://huggingface.co/datasets/CashlessConsumer/chennai-rain-gauges).
- SAR limits in the built-up city (layover, shadow) — [Sundaram et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11356-023-26467-7).

### Possible independent ground truth for Chennai
1. **CFM-DSS `crowdsourced` layer, via the public WFS or the HF mirror.** It has time-stamped, graded (1–6) point reports, including negatives, over 2022–2025. It probably does not cover December 2023 itself (see Gaps); the Dec-2023 archive in `chennai-rain-gauges` may contain it. It is independent of OpenCity's 2015 KMLs, so it could test both calibration and decay (age) behaviour. Caveats: test entries, out-of-area points, an unofficial mirror, and an unknown licence.
2. **chennaiwaterlogging.org (IIT-M) reports for Dec 2023.** About 1,200 depth reports on 4–5 Dec with location and time. Public display covers only a 24-h window, so the archive must be requested from the IIT-M team (Prof. Balaji Narasimhan's group).
3. **NCCR's more than 400 field flood-level points for 2015**, and the IIT-M planned high-water-mark survey (about 2,000 points, 10 per ward) for 2023. These are depth ground truth without fine timing; request from NCCR and IIT-M.
4. **Karmegam et al. 2021** (95 points with field validation) and **Ajith Kumar et al. 2026** (Adyar floodplain depth measurements). Check supplementary data or ask the authors.
5. **osm-in Flooded Streets 2015 GeoJSON.** It is OSM-way-level and joinable to CityPulse's OSM graph by `osm_id`. **Caution:** CityPulse's OpenCity 2015 KMLs (5,052 crowd observations) may derive from this same tool or from The News Minute layer, so it may **not** be independent. Check the overlap before using it.
6. **Natarajan et al. 2021: 123 historic flood locations**, usable for a susceptibility-prior check only (not time-resolved).
7. **GCC subway boom-barrier logs and the 40 flood-meter CCTVs** would be ideal binary passability ground truth with timestamps, but I found no evidence they are published.

### Inferences
- The CFM-DSS layer's sid values span roughly 370 to 1,400, which suggests on the order of a thousand reports. That is small but uniquely contains negative reports, which matter for the council's "traversal-silence and negative observation" pivot. This is an estimate; I did not count the rows.
- No source I found gives time-stamped road closures for 2015. The single proxy timestamp in the CityPulse replay corpus reflects a real data limit, not a pipeline bug.

### Gaps
- No paper or dataset found with time-stamped road closures or depths along Chennai roads for 2021 (Nov) or 2024 (Fengal, Nov–Dec). Consensus quota ran out before these could be searched; Exa and WebSearch were not run on them.
- I did not open the parquet files, so per-event counts for Dec 2023 in the crowd layer are unknown, and the HF mirror's licence was not stated on the pages I read.
- The Deepthi et al. 2025 abstract attributes the 17 Dec 2023 southern Tamil Nadu rain to Cyclone Michaung, whose landfall was in Andhra Pradesh on 5 Dec (per Mishra 2024's description of 4–6 Dec). I did not verify the meteorological attribution, so treat it cautiously.

## Q3. What drives Chennai's flooding (reservoir releases, Adyar/Cooum/Kosasthalaiyar, Pallikaranai, urbanisation)

### Takeaway
The evidence points to compound drivers. These are extreme northeast-monsoon or cyclonic rain (El Niño and positive IOD years), unmanaged reservoir releases (Chembarambakkam into the Adyar in 2015), encroachment and loss of water bodies and wetlands (Pallikaranai), inadequate storm-water drains, and a low, flat coast (CMA averages 6.7 m above MSL) with tidal backwater.

### Cited Findings
- Chembarambakkam had no inflow or real-time flood forecast. Releases of 29,000 cusec for 21 h on 1–2 Dec 2015, with outflow exceeding inflow, are termed "man made catastrophe". Storage was held below full tank level to protect illegally occupied foreshore land — [CAG 2017](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2016/Executive_summary_of_Report_No_4_of_2017_-_Performance_Audit_of_Flood_Management_and_Response_in_Chennai_.pdf).
- The CAG also found encroachment of tanks rising despite a 2007 law, GCC storm drains designed with an incorrect rainfall intensity, desilting not completed before the 2014 and 2015 monsoons, no basin master plan for the three rivers, and no Flood Plain Zoning law — [CAG 2017](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2016/Executive_summary_of_Report_No_4_of_2017_-_Performance_Audit_of_Flood_Management_and_Response_in_Chennai_.pdf).
- A premature 400-cusec release from Cholavaram into the overflowing Red Hills tank inundated Manali, Burma Nagar and other areas in December 2015 — [CAG chapter 3](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2016/Chapter_3_Management_of_Water_Bodies_of_Report_No_4_of_2017_-_Performance_Audit_of_Flood_Management_and_R.pdf).
- 2015 meteorology: strongest El Niño of the century, SST more than 1 K above normal, soil moisture near 70% saturation, and urban expansion from 1995 to 2015 — [Dutta et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dynatmoce.2024.101494). Urban land cover modulates simulated rainfall — [Panda & Rath 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00024-022-03113-w); [Rath & Panda 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00024-018-2069-5). Aerosol–cloud interaction matters too — [Paul et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-26-3749-2026).
- Future: Adyar flood depth rises 19.4% by 2030 and 60.4% by 2050 relative to 2015 under LULC and climate change — [Anuthaman et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.2166/wcc.2023.164). The upper Adyar is the origin of the 2015 and 2021 floods — [Kannapiran & Bhaskar 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gsd.2024.101085).
- Pallikaranai Marsh is rated "High" risk among India's coastal Ramsar wetlands — [Rakkasagi et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2024.130869).
- C-FLOWS models flooding from heavy rainfall, sea-level and tide, and the Cooum, Adyar and Kosasthalaiyar rivers. Low elevation plus high tide hinders drainage — [The Hindu 2018](https://www.thehindu.com/sci-tech/science/nccr-develops-system-to-estimate-predict-flooding-within-chennai/article25272941.ece); [Times of India 2018](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/chennai/ai-based-system-to-forecast-flood-in-city-week-in-advance/articleshow/65814959.cms).

### Inferences
- Reservoir-release timing is an exogenous, announceable signal. A router prior that ignores release bulletins (e.g. the CFM-DSS reservoir and bridge levels) misses the 2015-type mechanism along the Adyar corridor.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed quantification found here of how much of the 2023 Chennai flooding was reservoir-driven versus pluvial. Radhakrishnan 2024 (already in refs.tsv) gives regional discharge only.

## Q4. Indian warning and navigation products, and what they do

### Takeaway
India has authority-side forecasting (C-FLOWS, CFM-DSS, IMD, CWC, Google Flood Hub), alert dissemination (SACHET/CAP, TN-Alert), and crowd-reported waterlogging alerts inside mainstream navigation (Google Maps since Oct 2024; Mappls). None that I found computes a probabilistic, offline, explainable flood-risk route.

### Cited Findings
- TN-Alert gives forecast-based flood-risk alerts for the user's location from TN-SMART. It accepts feedback and reports, rings in silent mode, and has had location-based forecasts since v2.0 (Dec 2024) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/tn-alert/id1559849577). C-FLOWS was planned to be integrated with TN-SMART — [The Hindu 2018](https://www.thehindu.com/news/cities/chennai/cflows-to-forecast-floods-in-city/article24989752.ece).
- C-FLOWS has 796 scenarios, ward-level output and 10 days' lead. Accuracy is limited by sparse AWS coverage (2 IMD stations in 2019, with 30 more planned) — [New Indian Express 2019](https://www.newindianexpress.com/good-news/2019/Oct/18/chennai-becomes-first-in-india-to-get-an-intelligent-flood-warning-system-2049223.html).
- The IIT-M group shares 7-day basin- and city-scale forecasts from a 7-member, 4-km ensemble with GCC and WRD, integrated on a pilot basis with the TNUIFSL-funded RTFF & SDSS — [Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/iitm-team-log-flood-water-levels-chennai-residents-forecasting/).
- SACHET is CAP-based, piloted in Tamil Nadu, and implemented by C-DOT — [NDMA](https://ndma.gov.in/Capacity_Building/Ops_Comm/IT_Comm_Project).
- GCC's ICCC Early Warning System runs subway boom barriers triggered by water-level thresholds (17 subways), 40 flood-meter CCTVs and pump telemetry — [DT Next 2025](https://www.dtnext.in/news/chennai/advanced-warning-system-subway-barriers-in-place-to-mitigate-ditwah-damage-854965).
- Google Flood Hub started riverine forecasting in India in 2018 and reached 80 countries in May 2023 with 7-day lead — [Google blog 2023](https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/sustainability/flood-hub-ai-flood-forecasting-more-countries/). Urban flash-flood forecasts with 24-h lead launched in Mar 2026 — [Google Research 2026](https://research.google/blog/protecting-cities-with-ai-driven-flash-flood-forecasting/).
- Google Maps India added user-reported flooded-road alerts in Oct 2024 ("they can both see and report these alerts to take alternate routes") — [Times of India 2024](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/technology/tech-news/how-google-maps-plans-to-solve-flood-and-fog-problem-for-users-on-their-route/articleshow/113910447.cms). India had the most flood-related alerts on Maps globally in 2025 — [Google India blog 2025](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/explore-communicate/google-maps-in-india-keeping-you-informed-with-new-safety-disruption-alerts/).
- Hyderabad traffic police (July 2025) said Google Maps' "inability to detect waterlogging" and herding onto alternate roads worsened monsoon congestion — [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/telangana/maps-becomes-unintentionally-misdirecting-cops-1893093).
- Mappls lets users report waterlogging and flood alerts and advertises on-route waterlogging alerts — [Mappls docs](https://developer.mappls.com/documentation/sdk/widgets/mappls-post-on-map/); [Mappls 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mappls_waterlogging-alerts-activity-7490751087289954305-J_RR).

### Indian products that overlap with CityPulse
| Product | Overlap with CityPulse | What it lacks relative to CityPulse (as documented) |
|---|---|---|
| Google Maps (India) flood/waterlogging alerts | Crowd-reported flooded roads on route; alternate routes; traffic-police and NHAI closure feeds | No documented per-segment probability, report-age decay, offline routing or uncertainty-aware user classes. Police say it does not detect waterlogging itself. |
| Mappls waterlogging alerts | Crowd reports plus on-route alerts during navigation | Whether routing actively avoids reports is not documented in the sources I read. |
| TN-Alert / TN-SMART | Tamil Nadu govt, location-based flood-risk alerts, citizen reporting | Area alerts, not routing. |
| C-FLOWS / CFM-DSS | Ward-level inundation scenarios; reservoir and bridge levels; crowd reports | An authority decision-support tool; no citizen routing found. |
| GCC ICCC EWS | Subway barriers and flood meters (real passability sensors) | Data not published; no routing. |
| chennaiwaterlogging.org | Crowd depth reports in Chennai | For model calibration; no routing. |
| Google Flood Hub urban flash floods | Area-level 24-h flash-flood risk | Area-level, not road-segment level. |

### Inferences
- The defensible gap for CityPulse is narrow. "A crowd flood report shown on route" is already shipped in India by Google Maps and Mappls. The remaining differentiators are offline operation, calibrated per-segment uncertainty with decay, class-specific risk tolerance, and verifiable explanations. Fusing authoritative Chennai feeds (CFM-DSS levels, GCC barrier states) is a possible further differentiator, but data access is unproven.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether Google Maps or Mappls reroute automatically around flood reports, or only display them.
- The IMD or CWC urban-specific products for Chennai, and CAP feed URLs for Tamil Nadu, were not checked.
- iFLOWS-Mumbai was found only through NCMRWF preprint titles (10.21203/rs.3.rs-3552442/v1). Its official description was not read.

## Q5. Mobility impacts and Indian crowd-sourced flood mapping

### Takeaway
Indian studies quantify flood impact on roads through scenario models: more than 40% of road length immovable at 100-year rain; 65% of Dadar OD pairs beyond the base maximum; 29% of a city's area with severe mobility loss. Indian crowd mapping is mature in research for Chennai, Mumbai and Pune (Twitter, OSM, WhatsApp), but trust and coverage are open problems.

### Cited Findings
- More than 40% of road length becomes immovable in the 1-in-100-year event, with large drops in mean maximum speed per road class — [Singh et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijdrr.2018.03.017).
- About 65% of Dadar OD pairs exceed the base maximum travel time under disconnection; contraflow plus staging is the best evacuation strategy — [Lalwani et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1108/IJDRBE-12-2024-0130).
- Severe mobility impact over 29.35% of area, concentrated centrally — [Mukesh et al. 2022a](https://doi.org/10.1061/AJRUA6.0001256). A zone-wise resilience index is in [Mukesh et al. 2022b](https://doi.org/10.1108/IJDRBE-11-2021-0155).
- Crowdsourced Twitter mapping for Mumbai, validated against VGI and HAND; flood reporting declined after mitigation — [Tripathy et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.uclim.2024.101815).
- Chennai tweet pipelines are in [Sattaru et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12524-021-01376-9), [Pandey & Natarajan 2016](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICACCI.2016.7732236) and [Karmegam et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40677-021-00195-x). Multimodal severity estimation (Pune, Chennai, Kerala) is in [Kanth et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00477-021-02161-3).
- Crowd coverage bias: a GIS expert notes that only people aware of the tools and with access to technology report, so poorer areas may be missing — [Citizen Matters 2023](https://citizenmatters.in/iitm-team-log-flood-water-levels-chennai-residents-forecasting/). Low-income residents reported that red alerts did not become actionable warnings in 2023 — [OpenCity 2024](https://opencity.in/chennai-rains-and-waterlogging-datajam-jan-2024/).

### Inferences
- Indian road-flood studies use deterministic depth-to-speed curves, close to Pregnolato 2017 (already in refs.tsv). None uses report-age decay or uncertainty bounds, which supports CityPulse's novelty claim only at the fusion and uncertainty layer, not at the depth-disruption layer.

### Gaps
- No Indian study found that uses observed probe or GPS traffic data (e.g. Google, Uber or Ola speeds) during a Chennai flood to measure actual mobility loss. The closest observed-data analogue is non-Indian: [Rajput et al. (SSRN, Houston)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/ca1250b3796d5e4aa50c2df785b6ae80/?utm_source=claude_desktop). A broader non-Indian synthesis is Ermagun et al. 2026, *Sustainable Cities and Society* (systematic review of 61 studies). Neither was Crossref-verified here.
- The NDMA Urban Flood guidelines (2010) and TNSDMA monsoon SOPs were not retrieved.
