## Q1. Which of CityPulse's design choices have direct precedents?

### Takeaway
Most of the router's ingredients have clear precedents. These are: pessimism under thin evidence (offline-RL LCB penalties, robust and distributionally robust routing with confidence-sized ambiguity sets, worst-case CVaR when data are insufficient), a user-chosen confidence level, cost models that keep travel time separate from harm, chance or VaR-type constraints, and, most importantly, the fact that ALT/landmark potentials remain valid when weights only increase (Delling & Wagner 2007, already in refs; Nannicini et al. 2012 state it explicitly). Two things were not found as published routing precedents. The first is the exact combination: a per-edge Wilson-style upper bound on a hazard probability with an effective sample size, with z chosen per user class. The second is an explicit argument that confidence-multiplied penalties are risk-seeking under ambiguity. The decision-theory basis for that argument is Ellsberg 1961 and Gilboa & Schmeidler 1989.

### Summaries (2-3 sentences each; based only on what was read)

Robust, risk-averse and distributionally robust shortest paths
- yu1998robust, montemanni2004exact, sivakumar1994variance, chen2005pathfinding, xing2011reliable, gavriel2012riskaverse, jaillet2016routing, lo2006degradable: metadata verified, abstracts not read this session. They are cited only for what their titles state: the robust SP problem (Yu & Yang); an exact algorithm for robust SP with interval data (Montemanni & Gambardella); a variance-constrained SP (Sivakumar & Batta); path finding under uncertainty (Chen & Ji); most-reliable path with or without correlation via Lagrangian substitution (Xing & Zhou); risk-averse SP problems (Gavriel et al.); routing optimization under uncertainty (Jaillet et al.); travel-time budgets for travellers with heterogeneous risk aversion on a degradable network (Lo et al.). Read the abstracts before making specific claims.
- flajolet2018robust: Finds a history-dependent routing strategy that minimizes the risk of exceeding a travel-time budget. Its robust counterpart covers the case where arc-cost distributions are "only known through confidence intervals on some statistics such as the mean, the mean absolute deviation, and any quantile". It is evaluated on Singapore field data. This is the closest OR precedent for routing on confidence bounds built from limited data. [https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.2017.1662](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/opre.2017.1662)
- wang2020wdrsp: A data-driven DRSP in which the travel-time distribution is seen only through finite samples. It minimizes the worst-case α-reliable mean-excess travel time over a Wasserstein ball centred at the empirical distribution, where "the ball radius quantifies the level of its confidence". It reformulates as a 0-1 LP or SOCP and reports better out-of-sample performance. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/0fb8ad3047a056628ff92ee5d4806ffe/)
- zhang2018rsp: A robust SP model that needs only partial distribution information (support, mean, variance, correlation matrix) and hedges "against the risk from both the uncertainty of the random travel times and the uncertainty in their distributions". It also gives a primal approximation that solves two deterministic SPs plus a mean-standard-deviation SP. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6c72e264eaba54608cbf569fda5950b2/)
- ketkov2021drsp: The decision-maker minimizes worst-case expected loss over an ambiguity set built from first-order moment constraints on subsets of arcs and individual probability constraints on particular arcs. The robust reformulation is strongly NP-hard, but without the moment constraints the problem is polynomially solvable. [Exa record](https://exa.ai/library/publication/b5tgtyh23bn)
- cheng2016drsp: Each arc has a deterministic cost and a random delay with known mean and second moment. The objective adds an expected delay-constraint-violation penalty to the path cost, and the problem is approximated by SDPs. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/42ba5ca822db56beb68eab622d1e33f6/)
- filippi2025survey: A survey of robust and DR shortest path problems (static, with recourse, dynamic; absolute and relative robustness) that classifies 29 papers. It is a good single citation for the field. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/52de5e3f235c52fa9926805ce7fbadbc/)

Hazmat (exposure-weighted, tail-risk)
- erkut2000catastrophe: Metadata verified, abstract not read. The title states catastrophe-avoidance models for hazmat route planning.
- kang2014var: A VaR model that picks a hazmat route for a specified risk confidence level. The authors conclude that "the route choice is a function of the level of risk tolerance of the decision-maker". [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8adf455c1db45093bded0b2963c51641/)
- toumazis2013cvar: CVaR routing with time-dependent accident probabilities and consequences. It jointly optimizes departure time and route and is tested on Buffalo, NY. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/78c1d89dcdce547e8689a890ae06f75e/)
- toumazis2016wcvar: Motivated by the fact that "historical data are usually insufficient to construct a probability distribution of accident probabilities and consequences", it minimizes worst-case CVaR over interval (box) uncertainty and proves coherence. The author PDF reports that WCVaR paths are "usually longer" and become more circuitous as the uncertainty budget grows. [Author PDF](https://comet.kaist.ac.kr/papers/toumazis2015wcvar.pdf)

Safest-path routing (crime, accidents)
- galbrun2016safe: Builds a crime-risk model per street segment for Chicago and Philadelphia ("relative probability of a crime on any road segment"). It treats length and risk as a bi-objective SP and returns a small set of trade-off paths. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6f30533d2d9255a78aba4e3f6aa3b8ce/)
- krumm2017riskaware: Estimates per-segment crash probability from traffic volume, road and environmental features. It models a route's crash probability "as a series of Bernoulli trials" and runs Dijkstra. The safest route is about 1.7x the duration of the fastest and "about half as dangerous", and the paper shows a smooth safety-time trade-off with several route options. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f7b5f51177f55f32a743a42be3e45b2a/)
- li2016roadrisk: Predicts a road risk index (RRI) from accident data with a neural model plus time-of-day and weather corrections. It balances time against RRI as a multiobjective network-flow MIP and runs a Columbus, Ohio case study. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/2f230e70e5e45ca29cf9bcd0610a2ee4/)
- ghoul2023safest: Uses a Bayesian hierarchical extreme-value model on drone trajectories (Athens, 102 intersections). The metric aggregates crash risk "along a certain route based on the duration of exposure to unsafe road conditions". Safest routes are 22% safer with 11% more travel time on average and identical to the fastest route in 23% of OD pairs. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/9bc1af399ac054289bd0624007a97fc0/)
- rohmann2026bayesian: Uses a Poisson-Gamma Empirical Bayes posterior crash rate per edge, with injury-cost severity weights and ML-predicted AADT exposure. AADT uncertainty is propagated by Monte Carlo. On 1,000 Berlin OD pairs, the result is up to 67% risk reduction at 89% average detour, or 54% at 21% detour for a "balanced" strategy. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/50b22e90795f532488c0ce6a3f9ee6f1/)

Exposure routing (air pollution, heat)
- sharker2014ape: Proposes an edge weight based on air-pollution exposure (APE), computed by geostatistical and non-geostatistical approaches, for least-exposure routes. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/a7ab98006f8655b5a9a2cf0b0512f136/)
- mahajan2019car: The CAR algorithm weights intersections by PM2.5 exposure on an OSM graph for Taiwan. Over more than 4,000 scenarios it reports 17.1% average exposure reduction for 2.4% average extra distance. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/da5f4978a4585613a0a638032ba25e46/)
- zou2020healthier: Healthier-route planning with time-dependent, grid-based segment exposure risk and dynamic Dijkstra, tested on a hundred OD pairs in Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei. Mitigation is much larger where exposure varies more over space (21.38% vs 0.86%). [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/a0d121b5b0ec5fcdac6c67c3742434e1/)
- wang2022activetravel: A low-exposure route method that depends on trip purpose and traveller demographics (Cangzhou, taxi-based mobile monitoring). Because older travellers walk more slowly, they can receive 22.9% (middle-aged) and 10.7% (elderly) higher exposure even on low-exposure routes, a direct effect of exposure being proportional to time. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d254b56c7f33582fa7a5535a57cec771/)
- choudhary2022multimodal: Adds real-time congestion and air quality to GraphHopper for Delhi and Bangalore, with fastest, shortest, least-exposure (LEAP) and balanced options per mode. In Delhi, exposure reductions are 42.73-71.50% (fastest to balanced) and 64.52-82.71% (fastest to LEAP). [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/72bbf2d652d755acbf063ef5ca92330e/)
- donaldson2026leicester: Pollution-weighted Dijkstra on OSMnx for 20 OD pairs, 4 time windows and 2 modes in Leicester. It reports both mean route concentration and estimated inhaled dose. Dose reductions were "modestly smaller … reflecting additional travel time on the pollution-minimising route", with a median detour of 0.94%. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/edc2a2c4217a592fa22dd61e9fdcb05a/)
- kolaxidis2025heat: A shade-aware pedestrian routing service for Heidelberg that reduces solar exposure "by 12 to 26%" in most cases (up to 90%) "with minimal detours". It also uses network analysis to find critical streets for shading interventions. [Crossref abstract](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/tgis.70110)
- wolf2025coolwalks: A route-choice model with a sun-avoidance parameter α and a "CoolWalkability" metric. It proves analytically that a regular grid with constant building heights gives no shade benefit over the shortest path, so benefits come only from geometric heterogeneity. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/5997e71ea8a45c0bb0b0775c56d99902/)
- melnikov2022thermal: In controlled experiments in a tropical city, distance walked in shade is "discounted by a factor of 0.86" compared with sun, and building shade has a stronger effect than tree shade. This is an empirical, distance-proportional discount on exposure. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/cac0359c270a53e6b8e1980f68ff8330/)

Learning, pessimism and robotics
- papadimitriou1991ctp: Metadata verified, abstract not read. "Shortest paths without a map" is the standard citation for the Canadian Traveller Problem (edges discovered blocked only on arrival). Describe it only at that level unless the abstract is checked.
- talebi2018online: Online shortest-path routing with unknown i.i.d. link-delay parameters, framed as a combinatorial bandit. It derives tight regret lower bounds for different feedback settings and proposes three algorithms. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/64e97fe0aec553f28e54649a5d03850c/)
- lagos2024online: Online SP for a logistics fleet with unknown arc travel-time distributions. It uses Thompson sampling with spatial (kriging-style) correlation, and the case study uses Beijing taxi data. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/de505d02a2015224a7c7ce274f24f3e6/)
- jin2025pessimism: Proposes pessimistic value iteration (PEVI), which "incorporates an uncertainty quantifier as the penalty function. Such a penalty function simply flips the sign of the bonus function for promoting exploration in online RL." It proves that pessimism is minimax optimal for offline RL without coverage assumptions. [INFORMS abstract](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.2022.0216)
- hakobyan2019cvar: Risk-aware planning with RRT* plus CVaR-constrained receding-horizon control. It argues that "unlike chance constraints, CVaR constraints are coherent, convex, and distinguish between tail events". [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/67eb77ab37605a5793ed8548f59fc3be/)
- fan2022costmaps: Learns a traversability costmap as CVaR (tail risk) at a chosen risk level from legged-robot data, and reports that this is more robust to outliers than baselines. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f9162dd8ac8f554c90f0473119c2bd03/)
- lamarre2026riskctp: Risk-averse Canadian Traveller Problem with CVaR-optimal policies found by AND-OR search, on Mars orbital maps with traversal probabilities. It shows "information-seeking detours" when traversabilities are correlated. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f705e70f8dad5025aa2f720f85d3a6a9/)
- koenig2005dstarlite: Metadata verified, abstract not read. "Fast replanning for navigation in unknown terrain" is the D* Lite paper. Cite it for incremental replanning after edge-cost changes, but check the abstract before making specific claims.

Landmarks and A* under weight increases
- nannicini2012bidir: The preprint body states: "if arc costs can only increase with respect to their original value, i.e., the value used in the precomputation of landmark distances, then the potential function associated to landmarks is still a valid lower bound, even on a time-dependent graph". The authors use free-flow (max-speed) arc times as the lower-bound costs. [Author preprint](https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr/~liberti/bidirtimedepj.pdf)
- efentakis2013alt: Cuts ALT preprocessing to "a few seconds", "making the ALT algorithm also suitable for dynamic scenarios, i.e., road networks with changing edge weights due to traffic updates". [Exa record](https://exa.ai/library/publication/761r314hv02)
- delling2007landmark (already in refs.tsv): its abstract states that ALT "still performs correct queries as long as an edge weight does not drop below its initial value". [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/1768570.1768577)

Ambiguity aversion
- gilboa1989maxmin: Axiomatizes maxmin expected utility, J(f) = min over a closed convex set C of priors of ∫u∘f dP. The key axioms are "uncertainty aversion" and certainty-independence. This is the formal basis for "evaluate under the worst plausible probability". [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/91ad298ab14a5d518e6be98e26f00e3c/)
- ellsberg1961risk: Metadata verified, abstract not read. It is the canonical source of the Ellsberg paradox: people prefer known-risk to ambiguous bets. Cite it for that standard point.

- Route choice under VaR "is a function of the level of risk tolerance of the decision-maker". — [Kang, Batta, Kwon (Consensus)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8adf455c1db45093bded0b2963c51641/)

- Empirical Bayes (Poisson-Gamma) posterior crash rates per edge feed a risk-aware router. — [Rohmann & Bachem, IEEE IV 2026 (Consensus)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/50b22e90795f532488c0ce6a3f9ee6f1/)

### Inferences
- CityPulse's p~ is structurally an LCB-style pessimism penalty, a count-based width ∝ 1/sqrt(n+1), applied to a hazard probability instead of a reward. The cleanest framing is "pessimism in the face of uncertainty" (Jin et al.) plus "maxmin over a confidence set" (Gilboa & Schmeidler; Flajolet et al.; Wang et al.). Claims of novelty for the idea of routing on an upper confidence bound are not defensible. A narrower claim (a per-edge binomial bound with decay-weighted n_eff in an offline, crowd-fed flood router with per-class z) may be.
- Because the penalty only increases edge weights above free-flow, the ALT admissibility result follows directly from Delling & Wagner 2007 and Nannicini et al. It should be cited as prior work. This corrects the team's "new result" claim (consistent with IDEA_AND_FACTS.md).
- The literature supports offering several options or a slider (Krumm; Galbrun; Ghoul; Choudhary) rather than a single hidden z. This matches a per-class z exposed to users.

### Gaps
- No routing paper found that uses a Wilson- or normal-approximation binomial upper bound on per-edge hazard probability with an effective (decay-weighted) sample size. The Consensus quota ran out (30/30), so this absence comes from limited search, not exhaustive search.
- No paper found that explicitly argues confidence-multiplied (evidence-discounted) penalties are ambiguity-seeking. This is an inference from Ellsberg / Gilboa-Schmeidler / Jin et al.
- No head-to-head comparison found of time-proportional and fixed per-segment hazard penalties in routing.
- Abstracts not read for 12 verified items (Yu & Yang; Montemanni & Gambardella; Sivakumar & Batta; Chen & Ji; Lo et al.; Xing & Zhou; Gavriel et al.; Jaillet et al.; Erkut & Ingolfsson; Papadimitriou & Yannakakis; Koenig & Likhachev; Ellsberg). Their summaries are title-level only.

## Q2. What do safest-path and risk-averse routing studies report about the time/risk trade-off?

### Takeaway
Reported trade-offs vary over two orders of magnitude, depending on how heterogeneous the hazard field is and how hard risk is weighted. Exposure-routing studies (pollution, shade) typically report 4-26% exposure reduction for a few percent extra distance. Crash-risk studies report roughly 20-67% risk reduction at 11-89% extra time. A large share of OD pairs often see no change at all.

### Cited Findings
- Safest route ≈1.7x the fastest route's duration and "about half as dangerous". A smooth trade-off is offered. — [Krumm & Horvitz 2017](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f7b5f51177f55f32a743a42be3e45b2a/)
- Safest routes are 22% safer on average at +11% travel time. They are identical to the fastest route in 23% of OD pairs, with 54% average link similarity. — [Ghoul, Sayed, Fu 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/9bc1af399ac054289bd0624007a97fc0/)
- Up to 67% expected crash-risk reduction at 89% average detour. A balanced strategy gives 54% reduction at 21% detour (1,000 Berlin OD pairs). — [Rohmann & Bachem 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/50b22e90795f532488c0ce6a3f9ee6f1/)
- CAR: 17.1% average PM2.5 exposure reduction for +2.4% average distance (more than 4,000 scenarios). — [Mahajan et al. 2019](https://consensus.app/papers/details/da5f4978a4585613a0a638032ba25e46/)
- Healthier-route mitigation of 21.38% where exposure varies strongly vs 0.86% where it is homogeneous. — [Zou et al. 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/details/a0d121b5b0ec5fcdac6c67c3742434e1/)
- Delhi: 42.73-71.50% exposure reduction from fastest to balanced, and 64.52-82.71% from fastest to LEAP routes. — [Choudhary et al. 2022](https://consensus.app/papers/details/72bbf2d652d755acbf063ef5ca92330e/)
- Leicester: routes diverged in 81% of combinations. Median NO2 concentration reduction was 2.89% and median inhaled-dose reduction 1.92%, at a median 0.94% detour. Dose reductions are smaller because of the extra travel time. — [Donaldson et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/edc2a2c4217a592fa22dd61e9fdcb05a/)
- Exercising travellers avoid 16.8% of commuters' exposure via low-exposure routes "but long detours". Middle-aged and elderly travellers get 22.9% and 10.7% higher exposure because of lower speed. — [Wang et al. 2022](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d254b56c7f33582fa7a5535a57cec771/)
- Solar exposure was reduced by 12-26% in most cases (up to 90%) "with minimal detours", but reasonable alternatives were "not available everywhere". — [Kolaxidis et al. 2025](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/tgis.70110)
- On a regular grid with constant building heights, shade-seeking gives no benefit over the shortest path. — [Wolf, Vierø, Szell 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/5997e71ea8a45c0bb0b0775c56d99902/)
- Worst-case CVaR hazmat paths are usually longer, and more circuitous with larger uncertainty budgets. In extreme cases the relationship can reverse because of network structure. — [Toumazis & Kwon author PDF](https://comet.kaist.ac.kr/papers/toumazis2015wcvar.pdf)
- UNVERIFIED-metadata leads (abstract read): Tshwane safest route ≈63% longer with roughly half the recorded incivilities ([Purchase et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f480230d7906575da26591f08edf2458/)). Thessaloniki: 4% lower AQI exposure at +3% distance over 3,000 OD pairs ([Salanova Grau et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/2ff82745a15f5ab58bb92bb7f27ae503/)). Berlin shade ABM: 12% more walking in shade at +2% journey time ([Verma et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/1235a3a20e3955e6b55e3d23758b31dd/)). Clermont-Ferrand: coolest route about 5 °C cooler but 72% longer for one OD pair ([Mansour et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/c756210772dc51d5a4d044a5595e173f/)).

### Inferences
- CityPulse's own sweep fits this literature. At commuter defaults (7/100 routes changed, +0.026 s) it sits at the "mostly unchanged" end. Its λ=5 point (Σp̄ 1.61→0.98 at 0.85% detour) is similar to the exposure-routing results. Its λ=20, z=2 point (Σp̄ 0.18 at 12.7%) is similar to the crash-risk results (Ghoul +11%). Reporting the share of OD pairs where the route is unchanged, as Ghoul and Donaldson do, is standard.
- Zou and Wolf both show that benefit depends on how heterogeneous the hazard is in space. CityPulse's prior (7,453 hazard-zone polygons with a 600 m influence radius) may smooth heterogeneity and cap the achievable gain.
- Donaldson and Wang show the cost of time-proportional exposure: a detour adds time and therefore dose. This is the exact mechanism CityPulse's τ0-proportional penalty encodes. It argues for reporting both a per-segment (Σp̄) and a time-weighted (Σp̄·τ) harm metric.

### Gaps
- No flood-specific study was found that reports a time/risk frontier for consumer navigation with probabilistic passability. Flood routing papers in refs.tsv (Li 2023, Liang 2025) are deterministic or rescue-focused.
- No study found that reports the trade-off as a function of evidence sparsity, that is, how the frontier moves with n.

## Q3. What evaluation protocols do these studies use?

### Takeaway
The dominant protocol is: sample many OD pairs on a real network, compute a baseline (shortest or fastest) route and one or more risk-aware routes, and report percentage risk or exposure reduction against percentage detour, often as a frontier over a weight parameter and sometimes stratified by time of day, mode or trip length. Out-of-sample evaluation against held-out data appears in the DRO literature but rarely in safest-path work, and almost no study validates routes against independent ground-truth outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- Sampled OD pairs: 100 ([Zou et al. 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/details/a0d121b5b0ec5fcdac6c67c3742434e1/)); 1,000 ([Rohmann & Bachem 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/50b22e90795f532488c0ce6a3f9ee6f1/)); more than 4,000 scenarios ([Mahajan et al. 2019](https://consensus.app/papers/details/da5f4978a4585613a0a638032ba25e46/)); 20 OD × 4 time windows × 2 modes ([Donaldson et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/edc2a2c4217a592fa22dd61e9fdcb05a/)); all OD pairs on a 102-intersection grid ([Ghoul et al. 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/9bc1af399ac054289bd0624007a97fc0/)).
- Metrics: % risk or exposure reduction vs % travel-time or distance increase; share of identical routes and link similarity ([Ghoul et al. 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/9bc1af399ac054289bd0624007a97fc0/)); mean route concentration vs inhaled dose ([Donaldson et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/edc2a2c4217a592fa22dd61e9fdcb05a/)); Pareto sets of distance–risk trade-off paths ([Galbrun et al. 2016](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6f30533d2d9255a78aba4e3f6aa3b8ce/)).
- Sensitivity and robustness: Monte Carlo propagation of exposure-model uncertainty and robustness to severity-weight variation ([Rohmann & Bachem 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/50b22e90795f532488c0ce6a3f9ee6f1/)); path length vs uncertainty budget and confidence level ([Toumazis & Kwon author PDF](https://comet.kaist.ac.kr/papers/toumazis2015wcvar.pdf)).
- Out-of-sample performance on synthetic and real road networks as the key DRO evaluation ([Wang et al. 2020 / ICCA version](https://consensus.app/papers/details/4f90de77e3fa5d77bf995065edf807d2/)). Field data from the Singapore road network ([Flajolet et al. 2018](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/opre.2017.1662)).
- Regret against the unknown optimal path, with lower bounds, for learning-based routing ([Talebi et al. 2018](https://consensus.app/papers/details/64e97fe0aec553f28e54649a5d03850c/)). A real taxi case study in Beijing ([Lagos et al. 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/de505d02a2015224a7c7ce274f24f3e6/)).
- Stratification by time of day and season ([Zou et al. 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/details/a0d121b5b0ec5fcdac6c67c3742434e1/); [Donaldson et al. 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/edc2a2c4217a592fa22dd61e9fdcb05a/)), by traveller demographics ([Wang et al. 2022](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d254b56c7f33582fa7a5535a57cec771/)) and by mode ([Choudhary et al. 2022](https://consensus.app/papers/details/72bbf2d652d755acbf063ef5ca92330e/)).

### Inferences
- CityPulse's re-analysis protocol (100 OD pairs, one reference belief, free-flow detour, a frontier over λ and z) already matches the field's norm. Adding "% OD pairs unchanged", "link similarity" and a time-weighted harm metric would align it with Ghoul and Donaldson.
- To credit z (pessimism) at all, CityPulse needs an out-of-sample protocol like the DRO papers: fit beliefs on a subset of reports (for example crowd only) and score routes against held-out reports (for example official), or against an independent flood map. A p̄-based reference cannot show any benefit from pessimism, as the team's own sweep found.

### Gaps
- No safest-route study found that validates against realized outcomes (incidents actually avoided). All evaluate against the same model that drives routing, the same circularity CityPulse's original harness had.
- No standard benchmark dataset for hazard-aware routing under sparse crowd evidence was found.
